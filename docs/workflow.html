nTop revolves a true B-spline — no chord polygon, no discretisation error. Control points are computed inside the notebook from live inputs, so one converted .ntop serves every design point in a sweep. The notebook reports volume, wetted area, cavity, CG, inertia and S(x); the loop consumes measurements rather than closed forms.

The claim under test

why stage C exists at all

Before this work no drag model in the repository could tell one nose from another at fixed fineness — CD_wave_body is a function of L/D alone. A shape study run against that model would have reported "no change" for entirely the wrong reason. Slender-body theory supplies the missing sensitivity as a dimensionless ratio, so the calibrated drag level stays with the correlation validated against real flight data and only the shape effect comes from theory.

What the ladder found

low fidelity, both vehicles
SV-1 · SINGLE STAGE10 of 10 met

−3.0 kg · +4.7 km

551.3 kg launch mass · 194.0 km range

The sizer converged nose_blend to 0.7, not 1.0 — it had the drag optimum available and moved away from it, because the forebody volume given up and the aft shift in centre of pressure cost more than the remaining wave drag saves.

The saving was not free. q_max rose from 193.5 to 199.1 kPa against a 200 kPa limit: lower drag buys speed, and speed is paid for in dynamic pressure.

→ 85-sample trade study · 0 failed to converge
→ analytic geometry would have called this design feasible — wrongly
IV-1 · TWO STAGE15 of 15 met

+22.3 % intercept altitude

19.95 km → 24.39 km at the same slant range

Slant range is pinned at the 100-mile requirement, so the drag saving does not appear as extra range. It appears as reaching that range higher and faster — which is what the binding constraints actually ask for. The spline is also feasible at a 36° pitchover where the ogive fails.

Also a trade. Aerodynamic lateral acceleration halves, 7.45 → 3.84 g, because the intercept is higher where dynamic pressure is lower. The requirement is met almost entirely by the attitude-control pack.

→ feasible region is wider, not merely better at one point
→ the vehicle is now more ACS-dependent than it was

Anatomy of a design point

from design vector to CFD verdict
  1. A design vector is proposed — diameter, length, fineness, propellant, and the shape blend.
  2. The nTop notebook runs headless against the cached .ntop; only the input values change, so no re-convert.
  3. nTop returns measured volume, wetted area, cavity, CG, inertia and S(x) — not closed-form estimates.
  4. masses builds a group-weight statement in which every line records whether it was measured or estimated.
  5. aero assembles the drag build-up and multiplies the forebody term by the slender-body shape ratio.
  6. trajectory integrates 3-DOF RK4 through every phase, with staging events as hard step boundaries.
  7. Constraint residuals drive a pattern search; the loop repeats until the design stops moving.
  8. The converged OML escalates to AFLR3 + FUN3D, where CFD returns a verdict the low-fidelity model could not give itself.
